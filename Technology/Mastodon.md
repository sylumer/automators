# Overview
Mastodon is a decentralised, open-source social networking platform that serves as an alternative to centralised services like [[Twitter|X (formerly Twitter)]].  Instead of being run by a single company, Mastodon operates as a network of thousands of independent servers, or "instances," that are interconnected through a standard protocol called [[ActivityPub]]. Users join a specific server based on their interests or moderation preferences, but can still seamlessly follow and interact with users on any other participating server in the wider network known as "*the fediverse*". This structure gives users greater control over their data, their community guidelines, and their experience. It is intended to provide a level of independence from corporate algorithms, advertising, etc.

# Features
- **Decentralised Architecture**: The platform runs on a network of independent, community-run servers, meaning no single entity owns or controls the entire network.
- **Ad-Free and No Algorithms**: Timelines are strictly chronological, displaying posts only from the people you follow, without algorithmically chosen content or paid advertisements.
- **Interoperability**: Built on the open ActivityPub protocol, Mastodon accounts can interact with users on other compatible platforms across the fediverse, not just other Mastodon servers.
- **Community Moderation**: Each server has its own set of rules and moderation team, allowing users to choose a community with policies they agree with, and instance administrators can block problematic servers from interacting with their own.
- **Account Migration**: You can move your followers to a new server without losing your social connections if you decide to switch instances - e.g. because you no longer agree with the instance's moderation policies.
- **Content Warnings**: Users are encouraged to place sensitive content, spoilers, or potentially triggering discussions behind a clickable content warning to give others control over what they view.
- **Post Editing and Higher Limits**: Users can edit posts after publishing and typically have a character limit of 500 characters, which is higher than many other [[micro-blogging]] platforms.
- **Privacy Controls**: Granular controls allow you to set the visibility of each post (public, unlisted, followers-only, or direct message).
- **Hashtag-Based Discovery**: Hashtags are essential for content discovery, as full-text search is typically limited to your own posts, favourites, and mentions. This is a counter measure to reduce the risk of user harassment.

# Notes
- Mastodon began in 2016, but when Elon Musk acquired [[Twitter]] in 2022 and changed policies, this resulted in a mass-migration of tech-enthusiasts to the Mastodon platform. It has not caught on with the broader populace as much, and so Mastodon is still often viewed as a nerdy or geeky platform, not being able to create the full network effect - likely due to more complex onboarding and use.
- A wide variety of client software is available as well as web interfaces.
- As an open standard, it lends itself to web automation as well as automations driven via local user client applications.
